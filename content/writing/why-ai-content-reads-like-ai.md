Give a model your brand voice document, your deck, a year of customer calls, and
the transcript of every sales demo. Ask for a LinkedIn post. What comes back will
be grammatical, on topic, and completely unusable.

This is not a prompting problem. It is a material problem.

## The model has the facts and none of the choices

Writing that sounds like a person is mostly a record of decisions. Which detail
to lead with. Which objection to answer and which to ignore. Where to stop. A
model asked to summarise your company will make none of those choices, because
nothing in the input tells it what you are willing to be wrong about.

So it hedges. It covers the whole topic evenly. That even coverage is the tell,
more than any single word.

## Three habits that give it away

1. Every sentence is the same length, because nothing in the text is more
   important than anything else.
2. The nouns are categories rather than things. Solutions, workflows, outcomes.
   No one says those words out loud about their own work.
3. It arrives at a conclusion it has not earned, usually in the last line, and
   usually about the future.

## What we do instead

Start from the raw material, not from the summary. A founder answering a
question badly on camera is more useful than a polished paragraph about the
same subject, because the bad answer contains the thing they actually believe.

Then cut. Most of what we delete is the part a model would have written.
